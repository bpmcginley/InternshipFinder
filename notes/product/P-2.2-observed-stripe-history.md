# Top-up reconciliation from observed Stripe state

Stripe Developer Support confirmed that the Events API has a 30-day window, event-generation notifications have no documented delivery bound, and Stripe offers no archive of object snapshots or transitions. Stripe recommends reading the objects on a schedule or after relevant events. [Event-generation failures](https://docs.stripe.com/events/manage-webhook-endpoints#event-generation-failures)

## Proposed production behavior

Keep signed payment settlement and durable replay prevention. Use signed refund and dispute notifications to trigger a fresh Stripe read. Add a bounded scheduled check of still-linked purchases, rotating through them persistently so an old or unavailable record cannot starve other purchases. Reuse the current Checkout, PaymentIntent, Charge, Refund and Dispute identity, amount and mode checks. Reuse the existing purchase revision and digest so the scheduled check cannot overwrite a concurrent webhook, use of a run or deletion. A changed or unresolved payment restarts or clears the resolved-zero timer through the current reconciliation rules.

Store only the latest normalized evidence needed for reconciliation, its digest and when the check completed. Keep a bounded scheduling cursor and per-purchase due time if the existing indexes cannot schedule reliably. Do not retain Stripe payloads, customer contact information or card data. Before choosing an interval and batch size, qualify provider limits and demonstrate a full rotation on the expected inventory. Missed, failed or ambiguous checks break the observed interval and leave that purchase pending. Any snapshot policy must account for restores and schedule gaps.

A sequence of scheduled checks can establish what was observed. A dispute can reopen and return to its previous status between successful checks without a delivered event. A completed poll or a quiet event stream cannot mark the existing continuous-history coverage epoch as qualified. Keep the existing atomic retirement safeguard for independently verified history. Never substitute poll success for its proof. Keep ordinary account-deletion behavior and the approved refund and dispute effects.

## Owner decision and wording

The existing [privacy policy](https://internscout.org/privacy.html#topups) ties sales to cleanup readiness and automatic removal to verified payment history. Sales can open under that policy after a qualifying cleanup path is deployed. A launch that relies on manual review for history gaps, or treats a continuous run of scheduled checks as sufficient evidence despite potentially invisible transitions, needs an explicit owner decision, checked operational procedures, and matching published wording before checkout opens.

Proposed wording for a launch with manual review: “We check Stripe for refunds and disputes and keep the results needed to manage your extra runs. After a purchase has no runs left and its payment issues are resolved, it becomes eligible for cleanup after 400 days. If the checks leave gaps we cannot verify, the record stays pending for review.” Publish an availability change only once the review path, Stripe live Checkout and funded-run release checks have passed.
