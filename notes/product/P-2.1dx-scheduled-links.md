## Actual entry and input contract

The actual scheduled export admits only an exact `FOLLOW_LINKS_CRON` match with `FOLLOW_LINKS_ENABLED === "1"`. There is no default cron or added trigger. The existing repository clock, student billing, campaign reports, daily cleanup and separate Follow maintenance retain their original priority and bodies. A colliding link cron cannot acquire release inputs or send. Disabled or failed link work returns without falling through to daily cleanup.

`FOLLOW_LINKS_RELEASE` is explicitly supplied deployment JSON, bounded to 8,192 UTF-8 bytes, with exact keys `version`, `origin`, `deadlineMs`, `policy`, `predecessor`, `release`. Version is 1; origin is the existing first-party HTTPS origin. Duplicate decoded JSON members are rejected. `policy` has exactly the #629 sender/postal/cap/candidate/cleanup/transport fields. No sender or production values are selected here.

`release` has exactly `catalogUrl`, `labelsUrl`, `catalogSha256`, `sourceSha256`, `labelsSha256`, `notBefore`, `expiresAt`, `maxCatalogBytes`, `maxLabelBytes`, `maxKeys`. The URLs must be distinct canonical first-party HTTPS URLs without credentials, query or fragment. `predecessor` is either explicit `{genesis:true}` or exact `{url,catalogSha256,sourceSha256}` at a distinct URL. Its immutable bytes establish continuity; the current descriptor's time window permits their acquisition without renewing their old signup authority. Caps remain #629's: each catalog at most 1,000,000 bytes, labels at most 2,000,000, and 10,000 keys per array. There are at most three sequential GETs, no retry/cache/fallback, and at most 4,000,000 total body bytes across them. The positive explicit deadline uses the existing timer representation ceiling, not a selected production cadence or latency target.

The shared reader preserves the existing signup catalog parser API. Its new raw-byte result asserts only bounded acquisition, never integrity. The scheduled runner feeds predecessor `valid` sets, exact current catalog and label bytes and independent pins into #629, which validates canonical bytes, continuity, complete retained labels and rendering before ownership. All descriptor and binding values are captured before the first await; replacement cannot cross releases. Fetches omit credentials/cache, reject redirects and mismatched final URLs, and cancel/release failed or late bodies. Stream sizes are measured intrinsically before copying.

One nondecreasing wall clock and monotonic elapsed budget cover the entire acquisition and #629 hashing. The same budget also fences subsequent new work and final handoff. It is not an outer race around D1 or sending: admitted writes and transport settlement remain accounted for by the existing durable owner. Expiration during acquisition performs no D1; expiration after ownership can hold the owner. Restart, late acknowledgement or a new descriptor cannot reset that hold. The original release cutoff also remains in force. Existing confirmation/management alternation, budget, token and final consent fences are reused; no unowned sender path is added.
