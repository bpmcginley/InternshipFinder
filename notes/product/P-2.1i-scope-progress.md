## Storage and read contract

`follow_scope_progress` belongs to one `(subscriber_id, scope_key)` through a composite cascading foreign key. Pending scopes have no progress row. The first confirmation inserts its token generation and confirmation instant inside the existing D1 transaction. Concurrent/repeated confirmation and later owner linkage cannot rotate that identity or reset an advanced watermark. Removing and recreating the same scope in the same millisecond produces a different identity even if the subscriber and confirmation time are unchanged. Existing management responses retain their original fields.

Migration `0009_follow_scope_progress.sql` and its identical rerunnable legacy-schema appendix give preexisting confirmed scopes unique server-generated identities and their original confirmation timestamp. They leave pending scopes unset and existing progress unchanged. Invalid historical timestamps remain invalid; they are not silently corrected. New data has the same lifetime as its confirmed scope and cascades through existing deletion paths, without an additional retention period or history table.

`readFollowProgress(db, subscriberId, catalog, {upperBound, maxScopes, maxBytes})` is an internal read-only snapshot for a future compare-and-swap transaction. It reads one subscriber's confirmed scopes in canonical order, at most `maxScopes + 1` rows to detect overflow, sums their raw UTF-8 scope-key bytes inside that bounded SQL result and returns only NULL sentinels for the whole result if their sum exceeds `maxBytes`, and rejects an aggregate serialized result above `maxBytes`. Limits and the trusted catalog are supplied explicitly by the future caller. Only canonical scope key, confirmation timestamp, scope generation and watermark are returned; no email, owner hash or token is selected. All timestamps must be canonical UTC instants; `confirmed_at <= watermark <= upperBound`. Missing/malformed progress or over-budget data rejects the whole read; no valid-looking prefix is returned. Unknown/deleted subscribers produce an empty list. Deletion or scope changes after the read still require a later transactional identity/watermark check; this slice does not authorize queueing or advance progress.
