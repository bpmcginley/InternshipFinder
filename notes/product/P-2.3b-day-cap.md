## Storage and transaction

Test-only `employer_posting_day_caps(day, n)` contains no employer or visitor identifier. It is a short-lived overall-cap total, separate from retained company/day statistics. A D1 batch first increments a company only if the day cap exists below 50,000 and the company remains below 1,000; its second statement increments the day total only when `changes()` reports the first increment. Both commit or roll back together. Ordinary cap rejection changes neither row. No detached reservation or SUM scan remains in the count path.

Cleanup is one transaction: delete expired company/day statistics, delete cap rows strictly before the current UTC day, initialize the current cap only for an empty company/day, and SELECT its existence. Existing active caps are never reconstructed, decremented or reset by cleanup/restart. A newer cap prevents a delayed older cleanup from recreating a purged past-day cap. Repeated cleanup of an already clean active day performs no writes. Physical removal occurs on successful cleanup, not an automatic midnight TTL; later cron/global retention-health integration remains required.

A missing cap with existing company history is ambiguous, especially after owner deletion. Initialization fails closed until a fresh empty UTC day or separately reviewed operator recovery; it never guesses a total from surviving rows. Future migration must exclude simultaneous older writers and establish a trustworthy cap before enablement. An owner company/day-range deletion must preserve the active day-cap row. This protects the overall limit; exact same-company recollection/deletion policy remains a later admin-integration decision and is not silently solved by this aggregate.

Thrown errors or non-success batch results disable that counter instance until successful cleanup. A response lost after commit does not undo either aggregate or trigger an automatic retry. A known zero-change cap rejection keeps readiness intact. Existing stale-cleanup/day checks and independent isolate minute budget are preserved. These instance-local checks do not implement a cross-instance retention-health gate.
