## Receipt and handoff

The [Cloudflare Workers API](https://developers.cloudflare.com/email-service/api/send-emails/workers-api/) documents structured plain-text messages, custom headers and a Promise resolving to an object with `messageId`. The [headers reference](https://developers.cloudflare.com/email-service/reference/headers/) allows both existing unsubscribe headers. These documents support this adapter's contract; tests use a synthetic EMAIL object in Node and pinned workerd, never a real binding or provider.

`createFollowEmailTransport` requires literal `enabled: true` and an injected binding. Its returned function accepts only the existing rendered envelope shape, copies its own data fields and preserves all strings exactly. Structural bounds are a 256 KiB UTF-8 body, existing 200-character subject, 254-character addresses, 200-character sender name and 2,002-character unsubscribe header. This is a transport boundary, not a replacement for trusted rendering, consent, capability or URL validation.

After validation it invokes the captured `EMAIL.send` once synchronously. Only a Promise acknowledgement with one primitive nonempty bounded printable `messageId` fulfills as `{status: "accepted"}`; the identifier is discarded. Unknown, malformed, throwing and rejected outcomes use one generic error without provider details or logs. No queue, retry, raw envelope persistence or new visible wording is added.

A real timer and elapsed-clock fence bound acknowledgement waiting to ten seconds, optionally shorter for tests. EMAIL.send has no cancellation argument. Timeout rejects the caller while the instance remains busy until the actual provider promise settles. Both late success and late rejection are consumed without changing that caller's result. Concurrent calls reject immediately. New distinct work may proceed only after settlement, subject to its own durable claim. Future runtime must reuse one instance and preserve its existing durable global serialization across instances/restarts; creating another adapter is not a recovery mechanism. The existing claims keep ambiguous work spent. Provider acceptance cannot recall mail or prove inbox delivery; Cloudflare's [delivery lifecycle](https://developers.cloudflare.com/email-service/concepts/email-lifecycle/) includes downstream suppression/bounce handling and provider-managed retries.

## Qualification and remaining gates

Shared synthetic Node/native tests cover default-off zero binding access, exact three rendered envelopes, synchronous invocation, malformed input and receipts, single call on errors, busy timeout through late resolution/rejection, absolute deadline/rollback/rejected clocks and actual D1 management acceptance versus spent ambiguous claims. The final PR records exact results. Tests do not establish live Email Sending entitlement, receipt delivery, DKIM or quota.

Before any mounting, the operator must verify the actual Follow sender/postal values, sender restriction and recipient entitlement, shared quota with order reserve, unsubscribe availability and delivered-header/DKIM behavior. The caller must supply the already-reviewed envelope after its fresh database/clock/consent fence and retain global transport serialization. No sender, postal value, quota, provider setting, route or scheduler is chosen here. Independent exact review and owner release remain required.
