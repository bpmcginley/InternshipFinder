`runTopupHistoryScheduled(eventCron, descriptor, context)` accepts a trusted plain own-data descriptor with exact `{enabled,cron,limit,maxPages,deadlineMs}`. Enablement must be literal true; omission/off remains inert. The cron is one canonical UTC minute (0–59) and hour (0–23), followed by `* * *`. It chooses no production time. Page size remains 1–256 and deadline 1–60,000ms, with an additional maximum of 48 one-statement D1 pages (12,288 rows) per isolated invocation. The existing drain statuses and acknowledged aggregate semantics are preserved.

The trusted own-data context contains `{excludedCrons,getDb,monotonicNow}`. The host must supply the complete current earlier-lane exclusions, including disabled reserved lanes. The bounded array permits at most 16 expressions of at most 128 characters. Fixed values, wildcard fields, comma lists and finite minute/hour ranges are checked semantically; unsupported steps, named/calendar forms or malformed exclusions conservatively hold. Thus `4 7 * * *` conflicts with `4,34 * * * *` despite unequal strings. Missing/unverifiable/colliding configuration performs no DB acquisition. These technical constraints do not assert account entitlement or authorize a Cron trigger.

The database is acquired lazily inside drain preparation, after its single monotonic budget starts. Acquisition/preparation time cannot extend that budget; no second clock or timer resets it. The supplier must be synchronous. Recognizable Promise prototype chains (including subclasses) are rejected before inspecting binding methods, so attached `prepare`/`batch` functions cannot turn an ordinary Promise into a DB port. Ordinary native rejection is observed without foreign thenable adoption or constructor/species/then getters. Prototype inspection is bounded to 16 levels; unsupported deep chains hold, and exotic rejection handling remains supplier-owned. The drain retains its exact late-acknowledgement, partial-cap, unknown-acknowledgement and no-retry behavior. No logs contain data.
