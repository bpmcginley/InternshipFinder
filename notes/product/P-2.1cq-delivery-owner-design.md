# P-2.1cq Private Follow delivery owner design

Proposed November 12, 2026. Draft [#563](https://github.com/bpmcginley/InternshipFinder/pull/563), design only, needs-bruce. No schema, implementation, schedule, provider call or production setting is changed. No new user-visible words are proposed. This addresses the owner boundary of [#560](https://github.com/bpmcginley/InternshipFinder/pull/560), not permission to send email.

## Existing guarantees and measured gap

The audit used main `e99a56fdc781c05b7c80be898a1131f45059c51e` and exact #560 `40f07518333e8d40615f6a644552c16202a664bc`. Its runtime, transport, dispatch, claim and budget sources are unchanged on this note's base, `7b7c16179ca17cba8128be21407d5eaab9947840`.

| Source at the audited revision | What it supplies |
| --- | --- |
| `worker/src/follow-confirmation-claim.js:79` and `follow-management-claim.js:85` | Atomic one-job claims and budget debit. A lost claim acknowledgement is spent; raw links cannot be reconstructed for retry. Different jobs remain independently claimable. |
| `worker/src/follow-send-budget.js:11` and migration `0016_follow_send_budget.sql` | A daily aggregate and monotonic clock fence, with no active owner or continuation. A quota is not mutual exclusion. |
| `worker/src/follow-outbox.js:73` and `:278` | Digest-only leases. Expiry can mark an attempt uncertain and release its lease; it cannot establish that a provider handoff stopped and does not cover confirmation/management jobs. |
| `worker/src/follow-link-runtime.js:84` and `:156` | Instance-local busy and held flags. The uncertainty hold survives a late response only in that instance. |
| `worker/src/follow-link-dispatch.js:49` and `:179` | Caller-owned per-kind cursor and next turn, including a capacity-blocked turn. No persistence. |
| `worker/src/follow-email-transport.js:113` | A bounded acknowledgement wait, with no provider AbortSignal contract. Timeout is not cancellation or proof of non-delivery. |

Three additional synthetic probes used the exact runtime, actual claim/dispatch SQL and the existing Node SQLite D1 adapter. Only fake recipients, injected bindings and an in-memory database were used; global fetch was denied. The eight existing runtime scenarios also passed.

| Probe | Measured outcome |
| --- | --- |
| Two independently constructed runtimes share one DB; hold the first binding promise while starting the second on another job. | Two simultaneous handoffs, two correctly charged budget attempts. Per-job safety did not provide global serialization. |
| Reject the first acknowledgement as ambiguous; run the same instance, then construct a fresh instance over the same DB. | The original instance returned held; the new instance processed a different job. The original job stayed claimed. |
| Run one candidate, retain the returned management turn only in test memory, then restart without passing it. | Confirmation was selected twice instead of alternating to management. Durable claims still excluded the first job. |

These are measured JavaScript/SQLite results, not native Workerd multi-isolate, process-crash, provider-delivery or production proofs. They do not demonstrate duplicate sends of the same claimed job. Native two-owner and restart qualification is required for any implementation.

Engineering separately reported a synthetic SQLite snapshot replay against the same exact #560 runtime: after a confirmed accepted management handoff, the live database did not hand off the same job again, but restoring its pre-claim snapshot did. This author did not rerun that probe; no transferable test artifact is attached. It supports the restore acceptance case below, not a production delivery or native crash guarantee.

## Minimum proposed private record

Use one dedicated D1 control record for the Follow email lane, separate from the attempt budget and all subscriber outboxes. Initial users are confirmation and management delivery. Any future digest sender must join the same authority before it can use the shared Follow transport. Order mail and other products are outside this ownership lane. That exclusion does not reserve provider capacity: a separately qualified shared provider budget must account for those sends and preserve their reserve before any Follow activation.

The proposed bounded record contains a format version, a unique acquisition generation, a phase (`ready`, `running` or `held`), and exactly the dispatch continuation: up to two nullable pairs of outbox job ID and expiry, plus the confirmation/management next turn. The generation is concurrency bookkeeping, not a credential or recipient identifier. No email address, subscriber/account ID, scope, raw link/token, rendered message, provider message ID, delivery log or copied payload is added. There is one current record, not an accumulating history. Job IDs can still refer to personal records while those records exist, so this is explicitly new private persistence requiring approval.

This is a proposed shape, not DDL. The existing budget row and digest leases must not be repurposed. No database service identifier, binding value or production limit is selected here.

## Acquisition, continuation and conservative recovery

1. A conditional atomic write acquires only the exact ready record and establishes a fresh generation as running. If its acknowledgement is lost, that caller stops and never invokes the runtime or retries acquisition as though it owns the record. A committed running record blocks another caller; an acquisition that did not commit does not itself turn a still-ready record into a global hold. Acquire before invoking #560, preserving the delivery helpers' existing final consent/expiry check followed immediately by synchronous transport. Do not insert an awaited owner write after that final check.
2. Invoke one explicitly bounded runtime pass under that ownership. Keep its existing per-job claims, budget, policy checks and transport unchanged. A proposed implementation must prove that each eligible entry path uses the owner; an owner record alone cannot serialize a bypassing caller.
3. A same-generation conditional completion saves only a strictly validated returned continuation. Missing dispatch output grants no cursor advance. Only a result meeting the clean evidence table below may atomically save progress and return to ready; the status string alone is insufficient. A sending-off maintenance result must not clear a pre-existing hold or invent progress. A stale completion cannot release a replacement generation.
4. An uncertain handoff or unexplained completion never authorizes a write to ready. A lost acknowledgement of the hold write leaves either held or the prior running record, both closed to another sender. Do not automatically reclaim based on elapsed time, an expired link, queue cleanup, a daily budget reset, process restart or a late provider result. The provider can accept a message before its acknowledgement is lost. Recreating #560 is not recovery, and the same spent job must never be retried.
5. A lost acknowledgement of a **clean completion CAS** is different. Clean evidence must already have been established before attempting the atomic progress-and-ready write. The old caller stops without retrying or writing a compensating hold. If the CAS actually committed, the next caller may observe ready and acquire normally because that clean evidence authorized the committed transition. If it did not commit, running remains closed. Do not claim that every lost completion acknowledgement creates a global hold, or let an old caller overwrite a successor's record.
6. Releasing a hold requires an explicitly approved reconciliation procedure establishing both that the old invocation cannot resume and that its unresolved external handoff is settled or incapable of later delivery. Changing a generation only fences future database writes; it does not cancel an old provider request or prevent an old process from making a later handoff. This evidence and reset procedure do not exist yet. If they cannot be established, sending stays off. Do not infer safe recovery from a timeout or an operator's wish to resume.

The completion validator must require an exact known result shape, matching outer/dispatch status, successful cleanup, and a valid bounded continuation with exactly the two nullable cursors and known next turn. Counts must contain exactly `examined`, `accepted`, `uncertain`, `unavailable` and `skipped`, all nonnegative safe integers bounded by the invocation candidate limit. Source `follow-link-dispatch.js` can finish as `processed` after incrementing `unavailable`; that is not clean evidence.

| Result and evidence | Permitted owner transition |
| --- | --- |
| `processed`, with zero uncertain/unavailable and `accepted + skipped = examined` | Same-generation CAS may persist the validated continuation and become ready. |
| `paused`, with zero uncertain/unavailable and `examined - accepted - skipped` equal to zero or one (the bounded capacity-blocked candidate) | Same-generation CAS may preserve the valid blocked turn/continuation and become ready. This proves no unresolved attempt, not future provider capacity. |
| `uncertain`, `unavailable`, any nonzero uncertain/unavailable count, inconsistent counts, malformed/missing dispatch or continuation, or unexplained exception | Never write ready or advance cursors from that output; keep running closed or conditionally hold the same generation. |
| Sending-off `maintained`, `disabled`, `busy` or `held` without a clean dispatch result | No completion authority. Maintenance may clear obsolete references but cannot clear ownership/hold or invent progress. |

Before persisting any returned cursor, the completion transaction must check the referenced kind/job still exists with the exact expiry and is not expired under database time, otherwise save null for that cursor. Deletion/expiry cleanup clears matching stored references atomically. These operations must serialize: cleanup after completion clears the reference, while completion after cleanup cannot restore it. A generation check alone does not fence this race because cleanup must preserve a running generation. Preserve the valid next turn; cursor hints never recreate jobs or authorize delivery.

The concrete recommendation for the first dormant implementation is an owner/continuation boundary with **no reset, reclaim or force-release operation**, plus offline tests. It must not recreate an existing running/held record as ready. A production scheduler, recovery authorization or provider reconciliation adapter is a separate package. No exactly-once delivery claim is made.

## Persistence and decisions for Bruce

Approval would cover **new private scheduler bookkeeping** and **availability lost in exchange for conservative uncertainty handling**, not new email copy, recipients or sending permission. The proposed persistence policy is:

- Keep the control fence while the lane is configured or an execution/hold remains unresolved. There is no automatic hold expiry. This can stop all Follow link delivery indefinitely after a crash, including one before any send.
- Keep at most two cursor job references. Clear the matching job ID and expiry at the existing queue deletion/expiry maintenance boundary, in the same atomic operation that removes the source queue entry, including cascades. A resumed read treats a logically expired cursor as absent even before that existing maintenance runs. Preserve the next turn, generation and ownership/hold. Do not add a new retention period, extend outbox/token retention or recreate a deleted subscriber/job.
- A durable hold blocks sending, not an independently qualified retention operation. A separately authorized, bounded, provably send-free expiry path may continue without acquiring or altering ownership, except the approved atomic redaction of obsolete cursor references. It must preserve generation, phase and next turn and must not invoke transport. If no such path is qualified, a held owner can delay that cleanup; this proposal does not claim timely erasure merely because a hold exists. No new schedule or cadence is proposed.
- Decommissioning must disable all entry paths and establish quiescence before removing the control record. **Hold all sends after an unverified restore or rollback**, even if the restored owner record says ready and no handoff was unresolved. A backup can resurrect a spent job claim and lower the attempt budget after a fully acknowledged, completed accepted send; replay could then duplicate delivery or exceed the intended cap. A restored record cannot itself authorize resumption. Production restore authorization must independently establish the required claim/budget history and execution/provider safety before sending can resume.

**Approval requested for the first dormant package:** the bounded private record, cursor cleanup tied to the existing deletion/expiry boundary, and no reset or automatic reclaim, accepting an indefinite hold. **Still undecided and production-gated:** backup/restore retention and independent protection of spent claims, budget and ownership; the evidence that would permit a held generation or restored database to resume; who may authorize recovery; and what to do when the provider cannot resolve an ambiguous handoff. A production recovery path requires those decisions and a reviewed runbook. Approval of dormant storage does not approve production restore or substitute for provider proof. No finite hold timeout or automatic retry is proposed.

Production also still needs verified EMAIL/sender configuration, approved postal/presentation values and public action endpoints, recipient entitlement, explicit shared provider quota/order reserve, a separately reviewed cadence and per-invocation budget, and qualified retention maintenance. The Follow-only D1 attempt counter cannot by itself establish capacity left after order mail or other provider users. Existing clock/financial cron branches are not an implicit delivery budget. No production values or defaults are proposed here.

## Acceptance before any mount

Use the actual local Workers/D1 runtime to prove two owners cannot reach two handoffs, same-job claims remain spent, and restarts preserve both cursors and a capacity-blocked turn. Inject crashes/lost acknowledgements before acquisition, after claim, during provider acceptance and after provider acknowledgement but before owner completion. Confirm no timer, restart, cleanup, day rollover or late callback releases uncertainty. Separately lose the acknowledgement after a clean completion CAS commits ready: the old caller must stop, a successor may acquire the committed ready record, and no compensating hold may overwrite it. Verify generation fencing, malformed stored state, `processed` with a nonzero unavailable count staying closed, and atomic deletion/expiry reference cleanup racing a still-running completion without resurrecting the reference. Restore a backup taken before a previously accepted job was claimed and its budget charged, with no unresolved handoff: all sends must remain held despite the restored ready record, unclaimed job and lower budget. Test restore/decommission authorization only against a separately approved production policy. All provider traffic remains synthetic until separately authorized qualification. Existing eight scenarios and three SQLite probes are a starting point, not acceptance of this unimplemented design.
