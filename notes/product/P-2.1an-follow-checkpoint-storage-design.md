# P-2.1an Private checkpoint storage qualification

Bruce approved [#358](https://github.com/bpmcginley/InternshipFinder/pull/358), including the exclusive 24-hour pending and seven-day accepted lifetimes. [#446](https://github.com/bpmcginley/InternshipFinder/pull/446) qualifies the separate SQLite runtime locally. Activation stays held because SQLite point-in-time recovery can restore database contents from up to 30 days ago and the provider documents no opt-out. The current reviewed runtime keeps raw checkpoint bytes in that database. [SQLite storage and PITR](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/api/sqlite-storage-api/)

## Proposed qualification

Use SQLite as the control authority and two fixed, private R2 slots for payloads. SQLite would hold the initialized flag, control and slot revisions, phase, opaque reservation and generation IDs, slot nonces, ETags and payload/checkpoint digests. It would also hold the minimal reconstructive record headers: pending candidate and predecessor commit/tree IDs and predecessor checkpoint digest, accepted commit/tree IDs, original preparation and acceptance instants, active deadlines, and completed replay identity and cutoff. Those headers and identifiers would remain restorable through SQLite PITR, including unpublished candidate IDs. Classify and explicitly approve that recoverable metadata before activation. SQLite would contain no listing tuples, raw first-seen stamps, per-row evidence instants, or raw checkpoint bodies. After active records and unresolved reservations are cleared, retain only the initialized flag and non-payload revision fences in current state.

Each R2 slot would hold one canonical common payload with the coverage boundary, source digests and exact sorted lineage tuples. Enforce configured maxima for each canonical checkpoint body, its R2 envelope and streamed reads before materializing bytes. Put a fresh, unique nonce in the actual R2 object body for every new payload envelope and every tombstone, including initialization. Persist the nonce and exact ETag after readback. The nonce stays outside canonical checkpoint reconstruction and digest calculation. Identical lineage bytes must produce a distinct object identity so stale cleanup cannot regain a matching ETag. Stage durably binds the inactive slot reservation to the expected payload nonce and digest in SQLite before the PUT. It fences the previous slot revision with a conditional tombstone, uploads conditionally, and verifies the nonce, ETag and digest. A fresh SQLite-clock CAS checks the reservation, old accepted identity and active deadlines before exposing pending. A late upload cannot replace an active slot and never becomes visible without that CAS. A lost reply holds until explicit reread. Create each fencing key with If-None-Match and keep its tombstone. Cleanup requires the exact retired control generation and If-Match on its R2 ETag. If a committed upload beats cleanup against the reserved ETag, reread the nonce envelope, match its nonce and digest to the exact expired or rejected reservation durably recorded before PUT, then fence the actual ETag or hold. This proof survives process death after a committed upload with a lost acknowledgement. A transient 404 does not prove a pending write failed or will not arrive.

For promotion, reconstruct the original canonical [pending v1](https://github.com/bpmcginley/InternshipFinder/pull/372) bytes from the common payload and pending header. Verify their SHA-256 against the exact pending generation. Reuse the typed [#378](https://github.com/bpmcginley/InternshipFinder/pull/378) accepted Git binding and verify the proposed [accepted v1](https://github.com/bpmcginley/InternshipFinder/pull/365) bytes and SHA-256 against the canonical reassembly from the same payload and accepted header. Promote by swapping pointers in the SQLite transaction, with no R2 write at promotion. Keep the same millisecond start/expiry rules and stale outcomes. Same-target replay requires the exact original pending generation and accepted Git identity before the earlier original pending or predecessor cutoff. It preserves the original accepted bytes, timestamp and expiry. No-op also preserves expiry. Reads recheck the exact control revision and fresh SQLite time after R2 I/O. Missing bytes and ambiguous writes hold until verified recovery.

A PITR restore to just before promotion can still match both R2 object identities because promotion changes only SQLite. For a controlled restore, use an external gate outside the restored database to disable reads, writes and alarm cleanup, and drain or fence in-flight operations before restoring. Keep the gate closed until an operator independently reconciles accepted Git and exact slot generations, verifies the recovered state and records a fresh external epoch before reopening. An epoch advanced only during known restores cannot detect an unplanned rollback. Automatic rollback detection remains unqualified until a non-rollback witness can be advanced and checked for every committed logical transition, including metadata-only promotion, without exposing an intermediate state. Matching slot ETags alone cannot authorize reopening.

## Retention and acceptance

App access ends at the approved cutoffs. Replacement of the current R2 object with a tombstone occurs through bounded cleanup and can lag during outages or retries. Bucket lifecycle rules act on whole objects and usually take up to 24 hours after their configured expiration. They cannot replace slot cleanup or remove the fencing markers. Private binding reads expose only the current object version. That property gives no assurance about customer restore capabilities or internal provider copies. Before activation, obtain provider confirmation on customer and provider recovery after overwrite, internal retention and cleanup lag, and verify the actual private bucket's caching, backup, lock, lifecycle and logging settings. The approved deletion cutoff remains unqualified because replacement can lag and provider recoverability is unknown. [R2 consistency](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/reference/consistency/) · [R2 lifecycle timing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/buckets/object-lifecycles/)

| Local qualification | Required evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Bounds and parity | Both original canonical formats and digests reproduce exactly through two representative 2.54 MB slots, including closed lineage. Oversize envelopes and reads fail closed. SQLite contains only the listed control metadata. |
| Timing and concurrency | Late upload, delayed read and promotion at either cutoff fail closed. Competing reservations, uncertain acknowledgements, stale cleanup and replay cannot replace a live generation or extend expiry. Rewriting identical payload bytes under a fresh nonce must invalidate the prior cleanup ETag. A lost upload acknowledgement followed by a stale reaper ETag must reconcile only the exact expired reservation. |
| Recovery and retirement | Exercise interruption at each storage handoff, exact tombstone fencing, cold reads and restore to just before metadata-only promotion with unchanged R2 ETags. Prove the external restore gate keeps all access and cleanup closed until exact reconciliation. Automatic rollback detection needs separate witness qualification. |

R2 alone compares committed object identity or prior upload time, so a delayed conditional write can cross an active deadline. A dedicated PostgreSQL instance can lock state and use a fresh database clock for CAS, and RDS can disable automated backups for a DB instance. That would require a new service and separate confirmation of remaining backup, snapshot, log and provider recovery behavior. No storage choice, private access, writer hook, deployment or sending is authorized here.
