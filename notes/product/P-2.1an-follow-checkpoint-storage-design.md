# P-2.1an Private checkpoint storage qualification

Bruce approved [#358](https://github.com/bpmcginley/InternshipFinder/pull/358), including the exclusive 24-hour pending and seven-day accepted lifetimes. [#446](https://github.com/bpmcginley/InternshipFinder/pull/446) qualifies the separate SQLite runtime locally. Activation stays held because SQLite point-in-time recovery can restore database contents from up to 30 days ago and the provider documents no opt-out. The current reviewed runtime keeps raw checkpoint bytes in that database. [SQLite storage and PITR](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/api/sqlite-storage-api/)

## Proposed qualification

Use SQLite as the control authority and two fixed, private R2 slots for payloads. SQLite would hold the initialized flag, control and slot revisions, phase, opaque reservation and generation IDs, slot ETags and payload/checkpoint digests. It would also hold the minimal reconstructive record headers: pending candidate and predecessor commit/tree IDs and predecessor checkpoint digest, accepted commit/tree IDs, original preparation and acceptance instants, active deadlines, and completed replay identity and cutoff. Those headers and identifiers would remain restorable through SQLite PITR, including unpublished candidate IDs. SQLite would contain no listing tuples, raw first-seen stamps, per-row evidence instants, or raw checkpoint bodies. After active records and unresolved reservations are cleared, retain only the initialized flag and non-payload revision fences in current state.

Each R2 slot would hold one bounded canonical common payload with the coverage boundary, source digests and exact sorted lineage tuples. Stage reserves the inactive slot in SQLite, fences its previous R2 revision with an ETag-conditional nonce-bearing tombstone, uploads conditionally, and verifies the exact ETag and digest. A fresh SQLite-clock CAS checks the reservation, old accepted identity and active deadlines before exposing pending. A late upload cannot replace an active slot and never becomes visible without that CAS. A lost reply holds until explicit reread. Create each fencing key with If-None-Match and keep its tombstone. Cleanup requires the exact retired control generation and If-Match on its R2 ETag.

For promotion, reconstruct the original canonical [pending v1](https://github.com/bpmcginley/InternshipFinder/pull/372) bytes from the common payload and pending header. Compare their SHA-256 to the exact pending generation. Reuse the typed [#378](https://github.com/bpmcginley/InternshipFinder/pull/378) accepted Git binding and compare the proposed [accepted v1](https://github.com/bpmcginley/InternshipFinder/pull/365) bytes with the canonical reassembly from the same payload and accepted header. Promote by swapping pointers in the SQLite transaction, with no R2 write at promotion. Keep the same millisecond start/expiry rules, stale and replay outcomes, and original replay cutoff. No-op preserves original expiry. Reads recheck the exact control revision and fresh SQLite time after R2 I/O. Missing bytes, ambiguous writes and restored metadata hold until verified recovery.

## Retention and acceptance

App access ends at the approved cutoffs. Physical overwrite with a tombstone occurs through bounded cleanup and can lag during outages or retries. Bucket lifecycle rules act on whole objects and usually take up to 24 hours after their configured expiration. They cannot replace slot cleanup or remove the fencing markers. Private binding reads expose only the current object version. That property gives no assurance about customer restore capabilities or internal provider copies. Before activation, obtain provider confirmation on customer and provider recovery after overwrite, internal retention and cleanup lag, and verify the actual private bucket's caching, backup, lock, lifecycle and logging settings. If raw erasure by the active cutoff is required, this proposal remains unqualified. [R2 consistency](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/reference/consistency/) · [R2 lifecycle timing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/buckets/object-lifecycles/)

| Local qualification | Required evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Bounds and parity | Both original canonical formats and digests reproduce exactly through two representative 2.54 MB slots, including closed lineage. SQLite contains only the listed control metadata. |
| Timing and concurrency | Late upload, delayed read and promotion at either cutoff fail closed. Competing reservations, uncertain acknowledgements, stale cleanup and replay cannot replace a live generation or extend expiry. |
| Recovery and retirement | Exercise interruption at each storage handoff, exact tombstone fencing, cold reads and controller rollback. A restored controller cannot serve or mutate until independently requalified against the exact Git and slot state. |

R2 alone compares committed object identity or prior upload time, so a delayed conditional write can cross an active deadline. A dedicated PostgreSQL instance can lock state and use a fresh database clock for CAS, and RDS can disable automated backups for a DB instance. That would require a new service and separate confirmation of remaining backup, snapshot, log and provider recovery behavior. No storage choice, private access, writer hook, deployment or sending is authorized here.
