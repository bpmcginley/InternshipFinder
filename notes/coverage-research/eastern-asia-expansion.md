# Eastern Asia internship coverage: feasibility and pilot

Research date: October 16, 2026. Research only; no production configuration, code, ingestion, accounts, applications, outreach, or deployment changed.

## Recommendation

An employer-direct pilot is practical after country-aware filtering and export support exist. Start with Hong Kong and English-accessible roles in Japan and Taiwan. Add selected South Korean employers next. Defer broad mainland China job-board ingestion until source permissions, access reliability, and local-language review are established. This is an engineering and operating recommendation, not a measured estimate of market size or a claim of comprehensive coverage.

For this report, Eastern Asia means Japan (JP), South Korea (KR), Taiwan (TW), Hong Kong (HK), and mainland China (CN). Hong Kong is tracked separately for listing locations and eligibility. Macau, Mongolia, North Korea, and Southeast Asia are outside this pilot; Singapore is not Eastern Asia. A listing's place of work, employer headquarters, ATS hosting region, and applicant eligibility must remain separate concepts.

## Evidence and limits

Official employer pages and official API/dataset documentation were checked through web research. Source-inferred findings below come from this repository. No authenticated feeds were accessed, no representative market census was run, and individual employer postings may close. Public readability or an unauthenticated endpoint does not itself establish republication permission. There is no claim here that visas, work authorization, or local recruitment regulation have been cleared.

## Repository fit and necessary changes

These are source observations, not measured production failures:

1. **Current geography excludes this expansion.** `backend/internscout/pipeline.py:48` drops normalized listings without `geo.in_region`. `region.py:106` uses `maybe_in_region` before detail calls; `region.py:120` classifies locations with US/Canada concepts. `geo.py:44` onward explicitly marks Japan/Tokyo, China, Hong Kong, Korea/Seoul, and Taiwan as foreign. Adding employer tokens alone will not create Eastern Asia coverage.
2. **Filtering happens before descriptions.** `sources/greenhouse.py` fetches detail only for locations accepted by `maybe_in_region`; Workday also uses region tests. An export-only change would leave descriptions missing. Use explicit configured country allowlists throughout discovery, fetch, normalization, export and UI, retaining US/Canada as the default until a pilot is deliberately enabled.
3. **Classification and search are predominantly English.** `classify.py:301` and `classify.py:560` recognize English student-opportunity patterns; `sources/workday.py:149` searches `intern` plus sector extras. Native-script titles can be missed before detail fetching. Add reviewed fixtures for Japanese `インターン`/`インターンシップ`, Korean `인턴`, Simplified Chinese `实习`, and Traditional Chinese `實習`; include non-intern graduate jobs and internship-program managers as negatives. Program, training, placement, and new-graduate roles need separate stage labels.
4. **Data model needs explicit dimensions.** `models.py:24` onward stores raw location, `state`, and `region_locations`, but no dedicated listing country, original language, requirement language, or work-authorization provenance. Add structured per-location country/subdivision/city, remote jurisdiction, source language, original title, and quoted eligibility evidence. Preserve unknown rather than defaulting to US or eligible. HK and CN remote jobs must not be merged merely because both say remote.
5. **Dates, compensation and deduplication need fixtures.** Workday's `parse_posted_on` (`sources/workday.py:75`) recognizes English relative dates only. `normalize.py:89` extracts compensation into a string. Preserve original currencies (JPY/KRW/TWD/HKD/CNY), pay period, subsidy versus wage, time zone and absolute deadline evidence. Keep employer requisition ID and source URL so bilingual versions do not inflate coverage; do not dedupe distinct city/eligibility variants without evidence.
6. **Existing employer scope is a head start.** `companies_seed.py:84` and registry contain Jane Street; registry also contains NVIDIA and Sony global Workday tokens. Reusing a board does not mean all locations or subsidiary boards are currently covered. Use a shadow country report to count raw, classified, accepted, exported, duplicate, closed and dropped-reason totals per market before changing publication.

## Suggested pilot and acceptance checks

Planning estimates below are proposals, not measured effort or guarantees.

- **Preparation, roughly 3–5 engineering days:** add country-aware fixtures and a shadow report, include country/remote scope in export/UI design, inventory employer robots/terms and board IDs, and sample original-language classification with competent language review. Avoid expanding Auto-Apply in this pilot.
- **Pilot, 2 weeks of observation:** use 10–20 permission-compatible direct employers across HK, JP and TW, starting with Jane Street, Sony, NVIDIA and HENNGE only after source-level checks. Reuse available ATS adapters and collect daily at conservative per-host rates. Maintain a separate opt-in geography so US/Canada coverage and ranking remain assessable.
- **Acceptance targets:** manually inspect at least 50 available pilot records (or all if fewer); at least 95% should be real internships/student opportunities in the stated market; no known CN/HK/TW or remote-country mixups; every published record must have a working canonical employer apply URL and source timestamp; sample deadlines and closures over repeated successful runs; preserve original language and compensation; report duplicates and missing eligibility rather than hiding them. These are proposed quality thresholds.
- **Next decision:** broaden to KR employer ATSs if the first pilot meets quality targets. Evaluate TaiwanJobs as a distinct licensed-source experiment only after validating freshness and extraction completeness. Work24 and Hello Work need access/eligibility review. Mainland China remains employer-by-employer until direct sources demonstrate reliable retrieval and terms compatibility.

## Operating risks and unresolved questions

- Language coverage is narrower than geographical coverage: an English-only pilot will favor multinational technology/finance employers. Report this limitation explicitly and measure discipline/employer diversity before calling it national coverage.
- Visa, student status, local enrollment, language proficiency and citizenship conditions are job-specific. Store evidence and unknown values; do not infer eligibility from page language, employer travel assistance or “remote.” This report does not provide immigration advice.
- Public job aggregation and automated application assistance may be treated differently by local rules and source contracts. Before a public market launch, obtain a qualified assessment of the actual operating model and reuse rights. No particular licensing obligation is asserted here.
- Captchas, sign-in, bot blocking, missing posted dates, regional availability and changing undocumented endpoints can increase maintenance. They are risks to test, not measured failures for every named site. Honor source restrictions; do not bypass access controls.
- No quantitative total-addressable-listing estimate is defensible from this research. The immediate measurable question is how many valid, fresh, unique Eastern Asia internships already flow through existing employer boards but are removed by region and language filters.
