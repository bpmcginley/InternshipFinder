# Student usability pilot, ready for owner recruitment

Prepared October 16, 2026. This is a session kit, not a completed study. No invitations were sent,
participants recruited, reviews requested or employer applications submitted.

## Five sessions

Ask five consenting students to try a 15-minute session: a first-year student, a student outside
software/finance, a returning visitor, someone using a phone, and someone using desktop Chrome.
One person can cover more than one category. Participation and newsletter signup are optional.
Use the existing campaign drafts only after the owner approves actual recipients and copy.

Open the site with `?nocount=1` for facilitated owner QA; this is a usability study, not a way to
inflate traffic, store users or reviews. Ask before recording. Notes should contain anonymous
participant labels and observed behavior, not resumes, email addresses or application answers.

## Tasks and success signals

| Task, in the student's own words | Observe without coaching | Success signal |
|---|---|---|
| Find two internships you could actually apply for | Field/state/remote choices, irrelevant roles, confusing pay or dates | Two employer destinations matching stated needs; inspect eligibility together, submit nothing |
| Save one to return to later | Discoverability of status control and persistence after reload | Student can find the saved choice again without help |
| Explain what Auto-Apply does and where it stops | Expectations, sign-in/setup confusion, trust in resume handling | Student understands the final submission remains theirs; installation is optional |
| Find the weekly email and explain what will arrive | Whether same-for-everyone, Monday timing, confirmation and unsubscribe are clear | Student can describe the offer; do not subscribe on their behalf |
| Find help after a pretend sign-in failure | Discoverability of install troubleshooting and private feedback | Student reaches support and knows to omit private application data |

Use synthetic offline forms and a fictional profile for any extension demonstration. Keep real
Google/Microsoft sign-in and an ordinary extension update as separate owner-controlled release checks.

## Record and decide

For each task record independent completion, prompted completion, or failure; approximate elapsed
time; the first point of confusion; and one short consented quote. Distinguish an observed failure
from a suggestion. End with “What would stop you using this again?” and “What did you expect here?”

Fix a blocker that prevents task completion first, then confusion repeated in at least two sessions.
Keep isolated feature requests in a backlog until their user problem is understood. Publish only
aggregate findings; five sessions do not establish conversion rates or broad ATS compatibility.
Do not offer rewards for positive store reviews or ask for reviews during a debugging session.

## Remaining product-data follow-up

The listing census in `listing-quality.md` distinguishes fixed stable-ID carryover from separate
canonical-URL copies and missing eligibility details. After the corrected normal export, repeat that
census and inspect a small employer-source sample. An inaccessible page means unknown, not closed.
Do not merge conflicting eligibility claims or delete roles merely because a review tool cannot open them.
